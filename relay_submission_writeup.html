1. Why We Built Relay

In residential communities, urgent requests for help happen daily. An elderly neighbour runs out of heart medicine during heavy rain. A single parent needs an emergency pack of baby diapers for a sick child. A newcomer cannot decipher the legal wording on a clinic follow-up form.

Today, neighbourhoods try to solve this with WhatsApp or Telegram group chats. These groups quickly turn into noise. Requests get buried beneath unrelated chatter, nobody knows if an item has already been picked up, and volunteers hesitate to jump in because there is no accountability or privacy. Requesters are left anxious, unsure if anyone is coming.

We built Relay to give neighbourhoods a dispatch-style chat structure. When a resident posts a request, Relay opens a dedicated ticket. Volunteers see a live feed of active needs, one person claims it, and the app connects them in a dedicated 1-on-1 chat room powered by Nexconn. Both sides see an unambiguous four-stage progression (Open → Claimed → In Progress → Resolved) so that nothing falls through the cracks.

3. How Relay Works

1. Post Need with Urgency & Voice
Requesters pick a category (Medicine, Groceries, Ride, Translation, Fix), select urgency (High/Medium/Normal), and type or dictate details hands-free.
2. Live Dispatch Feed
Volunteers see a structured feed showing exact needs, street location, and urgency timers. One-click claim assigns the task.
3. Dedicated Nexconn Room
Claiming spins up a direct channel room between the two users. No personal phone numbers are shared.
4. Lifecycle Tracking & Resolution
Interactive status stepper updates live. Once completed, a summary and star appreciation note are archived in the neighbourhood record.

6. Business and Growth Path

Relay is designed as an accessible, localized community product that scales from residential apartment complexes to city-wide volunteer associations:

Target Distribution Channels
  • Residential Welfare Associations (RWAs) & Condos: Onboarding apartment complexes where residents already manage maintenance fees and community boards.
  • Senior Care NGOs: Partnering with local elder care networks to handle daily non-emergency medicine runs and check-ins.
  • Campus Volunteer Squads: University student mutual aid for note sharing, medicine drops, and campus transport.
Sustainable Unit Economics
  • Free Tier with Nexconn Chat: 10,000 monthly active users on Nexconn Free Plan keeps community operational costs near zero during early adoption.
  • Sponsored Neighborhood Nodes: Local pharmacies, clinics, and grocery stores can sponsor verified community dispatch hubs for a nominal monthly fee.
  • Privacy-First: No ads, no data harvesting. All personal data stays isolated to ticket duration.
